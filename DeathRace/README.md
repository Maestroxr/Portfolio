# Portfolio - Death Race

A top-down car combat racer in the spirit of Death Rally (1996). Four armed cars race laps of a closed track seen from
above: they shoot the gun mounted on the bonnet (a machine gun, a minigun, a flamethrower or a plasma cannon), drop
mines, fire homing rockets, pour oil, set off EMP blasts, lob mortar shells, burn nitro, and ram and grind each other
against the barriers. A car whose health runs out is wrecked: it blows up, burns, and is out of the race, and the car
that did it collects the bounty. Between races the prize money goes on repairs, upgrades, fittings, weapons and better
cars in the garage, and a career climbs a ladder of twenty drivers to its champion, in three leagues: the Wasteland
Circuit (The Warden), the Iron League (The Iron Baron) and the Hellfire League (Nemesis). Twelve cars on nine tiers,
twelve tracks. Quick races against the computer, two to four people in split screen at one device, and online races
against players on devices of their own. Two looks: Scorched (harsh desert daylight, rust and dust) and Nightfall
(night racing under neon, headlights and street lamps, wet asphalt). English and Hebrew.

Everything is made in code: the cars, the scenery, the track surfaces, the interface, the effects, the sounds and the
music are generated by the editor builders (no downloaded assets), seeded so a rebuild writes the same files.

## Playing

- **The title** shows computer cars racing behind the menu (a new track every couple of laps). **Career** starts a new
  career (a driver's name and colour, and the league: the Wasteland Circuit, or a later league once the one before has
  been won on this device) or opens the garage of the one being played; **Quick Race** picks a track (stars and best
  lap of each; tracks open as the campaign and the careers reach them), a car (any of the twelve), the difficulty and
  the laps; **Split Screen** sets up a race for two to four people at this device; **Play Online** opens the lobby;
  **Load Career** and **Save Career** are named saves (YAML files, BaseGame's saved games window); **Settings** has the
  volumes, the camera distance (near, normal, far), screen shake, the minimap, the laps of a quick race and the look;
  the flag switches between English and Hebrew.
- **Driving**: W / Up accelerates, S / Down brakes and then reverses, A D / Left Right steer; Space fires the gun, Left
  Shift burns nitro, E drops a mine, Q fires a rocket, R fires the special weapon (seat 2 of a split screen: the arrows
  with Right Ctrl, Right Shift, Enter, / and full stop; seats 3 and 4 the special on H and the number pad's minus; pads
  for all four, the special on the left shoulder; every seat's controls can be rebound in the split screen setup).
  Escape (or the pause button in the corner) pauses: Resume, the settings, or leave the race.
- **A race**: the cars line up on the grid (the people at the back in a career or quick race) and go on the third beep.
  The HUD shows the place, the lap and the times (this lap, the best), the health bar, the gun (its icon and name) and
  its rounds, the mines, rockets and nitro left, the special weapon the button fires next with its count (and the
  counts of the other two, small), the statuses the car is under (burning, slicked, jammed, each with the time it has
  left), the cash picked up, a minimap with every car on it, WRONG WAY, and a line for what happens (a wreck you caused
  and its bounty, a pickup, the fastest lap, a weapon with nothing left, being set on fire, oiled or jammed). When every
  person has finished or been wrecked, the computer's cars get a few more seconds, and the race ends: the finishers in
  their order, then the cars still running by how far they got, then the wrecks.
- **Pickups** lie in clusters round the track and come back a few seconds after they are taken: cash, a repair (a
  quarter of the car's health), ammunition (half a load) and a nitro charge.

## Rules of the fight

- **Guns**: one on the bonnet, fired with the gun's button; its rounds stop at the barriers, and a car carries its own
  number of them for the gun mounted (100 machine gun rounds on the Rustbucket to 300 on the Apocalypse; the other
  guns carry a share of that), refilled for every race.
  - **Machine gun** (every car has one): nine rounds a second, 1.4 damage a round, a range of 70 m, a little spread.
  - **Minigun**: sixteen lighter rounds a second (1.0 damage), 62 m, twice the spread; it carries 1.8 times the rounds.
  - **Flamethrower**: ten puffs of fire a second, each 1.0 damage to every car within 18 m in a 30 degree cone ahead
    that no barrier hides, and it sets them burning: 1.5 damage a second for 2 s (another puff starts it again).
  - **Plasma cannon**: three bolts a second that fly at 110 m/s (on top of the car's speed) for 90 m: 4.0 damage to
    the car hit and a burst of 1.5 m that gives the cars round it 40 % of that.
- **Mines** drop behind the car, arm after 0.6 s (a beep, a blinking light and a red ring on the road) and go off when
  any car comes within 1.6 m: 34 damage at the centre of a 5 m blast, less towards its edge, and a push.
- **Rockets** launch forward, accelerate to 70 m/s, home for two seconds on the nearest car in a 40 degree cone ahead
  (never on a car with a radar jammer) and hit for 30 with a 4 m blast; they burst on a barrier.
- **Special weapons** (R): the button fires the special armed, or the next one with any left.
  - **Oil slick**: a 4.5 m puddle poured behind the car that lies for 14 s; a car that drives into it keeps a quarter of
    its grip and half of its steering for 1.4 s and is thrown into a little spin; the car that poured it is safe in it
    for 1.5 s; a crash into the barriers soon after counts as the pourer's kill. At most twelve lie at once.
  - **EMP blast**: every other car within 14 m takes 5 damage and is jammed for 3 s: no gun, mines, rockets, specials or
    nitro (a burning charge goes out), 60 % of its top speed, sparks crawling over it.
  - **Mortar**: a shell lobbed on an arc to a point 32 m ahead along the track plus the way the car goes in its 1.1 s
    flight (a red ring warns where), bursting like a big rocket: 40 damage at the centre of a 7 m blast and a push.
- **Nitro**: a charge burns for 2.5 s: 35 % more top speed and 60 % more pull, flames from the exhausts; the nitro
  system burns 12 % longer and adds 6 % to the extra speed and pull a level.
- **Fittings** (the workshop): a **spiked bumper** (rams deal 2.5 times the damage and take half), **armour-piercing
  rounds** (every gun hits half as hard again), a **roll cage** (crashes into cars and barriers and scraping hurt 40 %
  less), **blast shielding** (mines, rockets, mortar shells, plasma bursts and exploding wrecks hurt 30 % less), a
  **radar jammer** (rockets never home on the car), a **field welder** (4 s after the last hit the car mends 1 % of its
  health a second, up to 60 %) and an **extended magazine** (half as many rounds again). The **gun mount** upgrade
  adds 6 % damage, 5 % rate of fire and 10 % rounds a level.
- **Crashes**: hitting another car head on or a barrier faster than about 7 m/s hurts in proportion to the speed;
  scraping along a wall throws sparks and wears a little. Armour cuts every hit by 9 % a level (the roll cage and blast
  shielding come on top).
- **Kills**: the car that dealt the last blow gets the wreck (a car that hits a wall within four seconds of being shot
  or oiled counts for the shooter; a car caught in its own blast counts for nobody).
- **The computer's drivers** follow a racing line worked out for every track, brake for the corners ahead, overtake on
  the side with room, avoid mines and steer round oil slicks, back out of trouble and fight with all they carry: the gun
  within its reach (the flamethrower close, the plasma cannon leading its target), a mine for a car close behind, a
  rocket at a car ahead (not at one with a radar jammer), oil for a car on their tail, an EMP when rivals crowd round, a
  mortar at a car 20-45 m ahead; jammed, they hold their fire. Their skill (0 to 1) decides how well. Easy races hold
  drivers of 0.1-0.3, Medium 0.4-0.6, Hard 0.7-0.9 in the first league (higher in the later leagues), and the
  champions are 1.

## The career

- **Three leagues**, each a ladder of twenty drivers (the player and nineteen rivals of its own) with a champion above
  it. A league opens on a device once the league before it has been won there; the new career window shows the three
  (a locked one says what opens it).

  Points 3/2/1/0, 6/4/2/1 and 10/7/4/2 in every league. A wrecked car gets no prize and no points but keeps its
  bounties and cash. The other two races of the day are run for the rivals too, and the ladder moves; rivals buy
  better cars as they score. Each rival drives a car of their own among the cars of their tier (the Jackal instead of
  the Hornet, the Mauler instead of the Viper, the Banshee instead of the Reaper: always the same for the same
  driver), and the same car shows on the ladder, the offers and in the race.
- **The rivals' kit**: in the Wasteland Circuit the rivals race the cars they always did (engine, tyres and armour
  levels with the days, spiked bumpers and armour-piercing rounds on the better cars later on) and carry a special
  weapon in the Hard races. In the later leagues they fight with everything the shop sells, by the league's schedule:
  the nitro system and gun mount with their other parts, the other fittings one by one, a gun of their own (Kid
  Kerosene's flamethrower, The Surgeon's plasma cannon...) and special weapons of their liking. Their drivers are
  better too (the AI's skill bands: Iron Easy 0.35-0.55 to Hard 0.82-0.95, Hellfire Easy 0.55-0.72 to Hard 0.9-1).
- **Tracks** open by the ladder in each league's own order: the Wasteland Circuit the first eight in order (Dust Bowl,
  Scrapyard Loop and Frostbite Pass from the start); the Iron League starts with five, Refinery Row among them, and
  opens Avalanche Ridge and Gridlock Boulevard on the climb; the Hellfire League starts with seven and opens the
  rest. Every track a career opens is remembered on the device for quick races and split screen.
- **The Showdown**: whenever the player starts a day at the top of the ladder, the league's champion (every upgrade
  and fitting, a gun of their own: The Warden a flamethrower, The Iron Baron a minigun, Nemesis a plasma cannon, and
  every special weapon) challenges them on The Crucible with the two best rivals. Winning it makes the player champion
  of the league; the career can go on after it. **Moving up**: the champion's window offers the next league (the
  same driver in the same colour, at the bottom of its ladder); the finished career is kept as a save named after
  the title ("Ada, Wasteland Circuit champion").
- **The garage** (the car): its stats (speed, acceleration, handling, armour, firepower, nitro, with what the next
  upgrade adds), its gun and the special weapon it fires first, its damage (repairs at $3 a point per tier of the car,
  all or half; a wreck is patched up for free to 20 %), and five upgrades up to the car's own maximum levels: engine,
  tyres, armour, the nitro system and the gun mount. The price of a level grows with the car's tier and the level (a
  tier 1 car's first level: $450 engine, $320 tyres, $380 armour, $280 nitro system, $360 gun mount).
- **The workshop**: seven fittings, bought once for the car (prices of a tier 1 car, dearer for better cars): spiked
  bumper $900, armour-piercing rounds $1,400, roll cage $1,100, blast shielding $1,300, radar jammer $1,600, field
  welder $2,000, extended magazine $800; and the guns: minigun $2,500, flamethrower $3,500, plasma cannon $6,000. A gun
  bought is mounted on the bonnet; a gun the car has (the machine gun always) is mounted again for nothing.
- **The black market** sells mines ($100, up to 8), rockets ($250, up to 6), nitro charges ($150, up to 6), oil slicks
  ($120, up to 6), EMP blasts ($300, up to 4) and mortar shells ($280, up to 5), all bought back for half; Arm picks the
  special weapon the Special button fires first (the next one with any left after it). And sabotage (a chosen rival
  of the next race starts at 55 % health).
- **The dealer** sells the cars of the league's tiers (above) and takes the current car in part exchange for half of
  its price and of everything spent on it (upgrades, fittings, guns); a car of a lower tier than the one driven is not
  sold, another car of the same tier is.
- **Standings** lists the ladder with every driver's car, points and a line about them, the champion above it.
- A simulated average player reaches the top of the Wasteland Circuit in about 25-40 races, buying a new car every ten
  or so, and takes the Iron League's title in about 40 races and the Hellfire League's in about 47 (the title follows
  a car of the champion's tier).
- The career saves itself before and after every race and when the garage is left; Save Career keeps it under a name
  (the list says the driver, league, day, rank and money). A save from before the leagues loads as a Wasteland
  Circuit career.

Tracks 9-11 came with the harder leagues (the Iron and Hellfire careers race them; the Wasteland does not): they are
16 m wide (the widest a track gets) with long straights for cars that pass sixty metres a second on nitro, and each
has one narrow stretch where the field has to queue up and the guns come out. They sit after the Crucible in the
table, so the indices saves keep never moved.

A track is code (`TrackLayouts`): the control points of its centre line, its width (wider in hairpins, narrower in
passes), its laps, the line, the pickup clusters and its set pieces; the race builds it at run time (`TrackBuilder`:
road, verges, curbs, barriers with their colliders, ground, line and grid, scenery scattered from the look's props,
landmarks, lamps, and the set pieces: fire pits, container stacks, tyre walls, and on the new tracks storage tanks with
stairs and red warning lights, pipe racks with grey, yellow and red pipes, snow-capped boulders, and pile-ups of
burnt-out cars, one of them still burning) in the look shown. An ordinary lap is 600 to 1,500 m (the arena 400-520).
Stars: three for a win on a track, two for second, one for third, in any race at this device.

**What opens a track** (quick races and split screen): the first three are open from the start; every other ordinary
track opens with a star on the ordinary track before it (the Crucible is skipped: a star on Downtown Carnage opens
Refinery Row), or when a career of any league on this device has reached it (each league opens its own tracks as the
player climbs its ladder; the device remembers them, `DeathRaceProgress.IsCareerTrackOpen`). The Crucible opens once a
champion of any league has been beaten. A locked track in the quick race says what opens it (the podium before it, and
the career rank, or for tracks 9-11 a career in the Iron League).

## Several people

- **Split screen** (BaseGame's local play): two to four people, each in a view of their own with a HUD; options: the
  track (those opened on this device, of all twelve), the laps, the car (one of the twelve for everyone, or a random
  car per seat) and computer cars to fill the grid to four (none, easy, normal, hard). Every car carries one level of
  every upgrade, three mines, two rockets, three nitro charges and one of each special weapon (oil, EMP, mortar). Race
  Again keeps the same people.
- **Online** (BaseGame's lobby and rooms; room options: the track, the laps, the car): a real time race like the Endless
  Runner's. Every car carries the machine gun and the split screen's load, specials included. Every device drives its
  own car and sends its pose about fifteen times a second; the others show as puppets that still fire, burn nitro and
  blow up from the flags of their poses. A car's own device judges every hit on it; mines, rockets, oil slicks, EMP
  blasts, mortar shells and wrecks go through the room's action log, so every device has them (a slick, a pulse or a
  shell of another device hurts and hinders only the cars driven here, and a car's statuses, burning, slicked, jammed,
  are its own device's, as its hits are); a pickup or a mine belongs to the first device that claims it; finishing is
  reported to the server, which ranks the room. When the first car finishes the others have thirty seconds. Private
  rooms, players at one device (companions) and tournaments are BaseGame's.
- **Shared screen and remote controls** (BaseGame's screen rooms: a device opens the room as the screen, the players
  join from their phones as remotes). The screen drives the seats of its remotes as a split screen race: a view and a
  HUD per remote, each car on the controls that arrive from its phone, the screen sending their poses, claims and
  results; a player on their own device is a puppet there, as anywhere. The remote panel on a phone shows the race in
  three lines the screen publishes a few times a second (rank and lap, hull, ammo, mines, rockets, nitro, kills, cash
  and the special armed; a finish or a wreck) over the pad: the stick accelerates, brakes and steers, four buttons fire
  the gun, burn nitro, drop a mine and fire a rocket, a fifth beside them fires the special weapon (a desktop remote
  presses the first seat's keys). A player may switch mid-race from the remote to their own device and back: the seat's
  car turns from a puppet into a car driven there from its last pose, with its stock reckoned from the room's log
  (`HandedOverStock`), or back; the race here never ends by its own clock while nobody is driven here (the room ends
  it). Tours: `hostscreen` (the room opens as the screen; it drives nothing itself), `joinremote` (plays through the
  pad from the seat's puppet), `joinown` (plays on its own device); `-deathrace-online-switch <t>` has a remote play on
  its own device from race time t and go back `-deathrace-online-switch-back` seconds later.
- **The server module** (`Server/Lib.cs`, database `skinnerboxes-deathrace`): the base server plus the race's tables
  (`RaceTrack`, `RaceRacer`, `RaceClaim`, `DeathRaceStats`) and reducers (`ClaimPickup`, `ClaimMine`, `FinishRace`);
  `Assets/Scripts/Online/OnlineRaceRules.cs` is compiled into the module and the game alike (the actions and their
  payloads, pose flags, claim keys, the number of cars and tracks a room may name, scores). Publish it with BaseGame's
  `Gamebox > Server > Publish All Games` (or `spacetime publish` here) after a change there.

Shared with the other games through BaseGame: the shop model and window (`Gamebox.Economy`, `ShopWindow`), the race
maths (`RacePath`, `LapTracker`, `RaceRanking`), the HUD pieces (`HudBar`, `Minimap`, `FloatingTexts`, `CameraShake`),
the low-poly mesh builder and palette (`LowPolyBuilder`, `PaletteTexture`) and the sound synthesiser (`SoundSynth`).

- The cars: `CarMeshes` (lofted rings, cabins, panels, wheels, fenders, guns, lights, rivets) builds every model as a
  `CarRig` (body, paint, head and tail lights, glowing lamps, wheels, and the points of the muzzle, mine chute, rocket
  mount, exhausts and beams); the first six are in `CarMeshes.Models.cs`, the six of the leagues in
  `CarMeshes.Models2.cs`. `CarModelView` shows a model: the paint in the driver's colour (matte on the Spectre,
  `paintGloss`), the lamps lit by the look, the glowing lamps (`glows`: the Spectre's strips, the Apocalypse's
  nozzles) brighter at night and on nitro.
- The interface pictures: `DeathRaceArtIcons` draws every icon (the nitro system, gun mount, fittings, guns, special
  weapons, workshop, league and statuses among them; the theme hands them out by name, `theme.SpriteOf("Plasma")`) and
  the five upgrade pictures; `DeathRaceArtPortraits` the twenty drivers' helmets and the three champions' (20 The
  Warden, 21 The Iron Baron, 22 Nemesis).
- The engines: `CarAudio` gives some cars a character of their own (the Jackal buzzes, the Mauler and the Goliath
  growl, the Spectre is hushed; the Banshee's supercharger and the Apocalypse's turbines whine over the engine, the
  turbines louder with nitro).

**Building**: `Death Race > Build Everything` (batch: `-executeMethod
Portfolio.DeathRace.EditorTools.DeathRaceBuildMenu.BuildEverything`) runs the art builder (surfaces, scenery,
interface, looks, and first the cars, items, effects and sounds), the prefab builder, the content builder (settings,
the twelve campaign levels from `TrackLayouts`, the content asset, the two themes, the launcher's definition) and the
scene builder; the other items rebuild one step. After editing `Localization/DeathRace.csv`, run `Gamebox >
Localization > Set Up Project`. Preview tools render to PNG files: `TrackPreview.RenderAll` (`-trackpreview-only
9,10,11` for some tracks, `-trackpreview-pieces 1` for a picture of every set piece), `CarArt.ContactSheet` (the cars
six to a sheet, all twelve at race height, their states and the garage pictures), `DeathRaceArtPreview.BuildAndRender`
and `DeathRaceArtPreview.IconSheet` (every icon, upgrade picture and portrait of both looks).

**Tests** (`Tests/Editor`, edit mode): tracks (layouts, the harder leagues' tracks, geometry, the builder and the set
pieces, unlocking), race rules, handling and the racing line, combat rules and damage on real cars (the arsenal's
tables, the fittings' arithmetic, the new guns, the special weapons, the radar jammer and the field welder), the
career (rules, ladder, offers, booking a race, the shop and its workshop, every league's career, champion and dealer,
a whole simulated career of each league, saves of every league and from before the leagues), the windows, split
screen and online encoding.

**Tours** (development players): `-deathrace-tour <folder>` walks through the whole game offline (title, settings,
career and garage in a Hellfire League career, a career race, results, quick race, pause, split screen with two and
four people) and writes screenshots and `tour.log`; `-deathrace-online host|join|hostprivate|joinprivate|hostparty|
joinparty <folder> [name]` plays an online race (run against a server of your own, `-gamebox-server
http://127.0.0.1:<port>`); the checks `-deathrace-drivecheck` (`-track`, `-car`, `-skill`, `-rivals`, `-level <n>`
for every part of every car at upgrade level n), `-deathrace-combatcheck` (every gun, the special weapons and the
fittings, with screenshots of the moments; `-full` runs whole races to count the wrecks, `-split 2` to 4 shows the
HUDs of split screen) and `-deathrace-careercheck` (the league picker, the garage on every page, the workshop, the
market, a Wasteland race, the champion's window and moving up, and a race of the Iron League) exercise driving and the
AI, the fights, and the career windows. The tours open the leagues by writing the titles to the device's record and
put it back when they end. Add `-gamebox-theme Nightfall` and `-gamebox-language he` for the other look and language.
