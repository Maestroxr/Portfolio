# Portfolio - Death Race

A top-down car combat racer in the spirit of Death Rally (1996). Four armed cars race laps of a closed track seen from
above: they shoot a machine gun mounted on the bonnet, drop mines, fire homing rockets, burn nitro, and ram and grind
each other against the barriers. A car whose health runs out is wrecked: it blows up, burns, and is out of the race,
and the car that did it collects the bounty. Between races the prize money goes on repairs, upgrades, weapons and
better cars in the garage, and a career climbs a ladder of twenty drivers to The Warden, the champion. Quick races
against the computer, two to four people in split screen at one device, and online races against players on devices of
their own. Two looks: Scorched (harsh desert daylight, rust and dust) and Nightfall (night racing under neon, headlights
and street lamps, wet asphalt). English and Hebrew.

Everything is made in code: the cars, the scenery, the track surfaces, the interface, the effects, the sounds and the
music are generated by the editor builders (no downloaded assets), seeded so a rebuild writes the same files.

## Playing

- **The title** shows computer cars racing behind the menu (a new track every couple of laps). **Career** starts a new
  career (a driver's name and colour) or opens the garage of the one being played; **Quick Race** picks a track (stars
  and best lap of each; tracks open as the campaign and the career reach them), a car, the difficulty and the laps;
  **Split Screen** sets up a race for two to four people at this device; **Play Online** opens the lobby; **Load Career**
  and **Save Career** are named saves (YAML files, BaseGame's saved games window); **Settings** has the volumes, the
  camera distance (near, normal, far), screen shake, the minimap, the laps of a quick race and the look; the flag
  switches between English and Hebrew.
- **Driving**: W / Up accelerates, S / Down brakes and then reverses, A D / Left Right steer; Space fires the machine
  gun, Left Shift burns nitro, E drops a mine, Q fires a rocket (seat 2 of a split screen: the arrows with Right Ctrl,
  Right Shift, Enter and /; pads for all four; every seat's controls can be rebound in the split screen setup). Escape
  (or the pause button in the corner) pauses: Resume, the settings, or leave the race.
- **A race**: the cars line up on the grid (the people at the back in a career or quick race) and go on the third beep.
  The HUD shows the place, the lap and the times (this lap, the best), the health bar, the rounds, mines, rockets and
  nitro left, the cash picked up, a minimap with every car on it, WRONG WAY, and a line for what happens (a wreck you
  caused and its bounty, a pickup, the fastest lap, a weapon with nothing left). When every person has finished or been
  wrecked, the computer's cars get a few more seconds, and the race ends: the finishers in their order, then the cars
  still running by how far they got, then the wrecks.
- **Pickups** lie in clusters round the track and come back a few seconds after they are taken: cash, a repair (a
  quarter of the car's health), ammunition (half a load) and a nitro charge.

## Rules of the fight

- **Machine gun**: every car has one, nine rounds a second, 1.4 damage a round (half as much again with armour-piercing
  rounds), a range of 70 m and a little spread; rounds stop at the barriers. A car carries its own number of rounds
  (100 on the Rustbucket to 200 on the Tyrant), refilled for every race.
- **Mines** drop behind the car, arm after 0.6 s (a beep, a blinking light and a red ring on the road) and go off when
  any car comes within 1.6 m: 34 damage at the centre of a 5 m blast, less towards its edge, and a push.
- **Rockets** launch forward, accelerate to 70 m/s, home for two seconds on the nearest car in a 40 degree cone ahead
  and hit for 30 with a 4 m blast; they burst on a barrier.
- **Nitro**: a charge burns for 2.5 s: 35 % more top speed and 60 % more pull, flames from the exhausts.
- **Crashes**: hitting another car head on or a barrier faster than about 7 m/s hurts in proportion to the speed (a
  spiked bumper deals 2.5 times the damage of a ram and takes half); scraping along a wall throws sparks and wears a
  little. Armour cuts every hit by 9 % a level.
- **Kills**: the car that dealt the last blow gets the wreck (a car that hits a wall within four seconds of being shot
  counts for the shooter; a car caught in its own blast counts for nobody).
- **The computer's drivers** follow a racing line worked out for every track, brake for the corners ahead, overtake on
  the side with room, avoid mines, back out of trouble and fight with all four weapons; their skill (0 to 1) decides how
  well. Easy races hold drivers of 0.1-0.3, Medium 0.4-0.6, Hard 0.7-0.9, and The Warden is 1.

## The career

- **The ladder**: twenty drivers, the player and nineteen rivals (Rusty Nails, Lugnut Lou, Sally Shrapnel, Crash
  Kowalski, Nitro Nell, Diesel Dan, Scorpion Sue, The Butcher, Lady Lockjaw, Johnny Napalm, Vera Venom, Iron Ivan,
  Blaze Monroe, Hammerhead, Mad Maxine, Viktor Vex, The Undertaker and others), ranked by points. A career starts last,
  in a Rustbucket, with $1,500, two mines and two nitro charges.
- **A day** offers three races in the garage: Easy, Medium and Hard, each on a track the career has opened, against
  three rivals from the matching third of the ladder, with its prize table, bounty and points. Prizes for places 1-4:
  Easy $1,000 / 550 / 250 / 50, Medium $3,500 / 2,000 / 700 / 150, Hard $7,500 / 4,200 / 1,800 / 500; a bounty for every
  car wrecked ($250 / $500 / $1,200); cash pickups worth $50 / $100 / $200. Points 3/2/1/0, 6/4/2/1 and 10/7/4/2. A
  wrecked car gets no prize and no points but keeps its bounties and cash. The other two races of the day are run for
  the rivals too, and the ladder moves; rivals buy better cars as they score.
- **The Showdown**: whenever the player starts a day at the top of the ladder, The Warden (a fully upgraded Tyrant)
  challenges them on The Crucible. Winning it ($30,000) makes the player champion; the career can go on after it.
- **The garage**: the car's stats and its damage (repairs at $3 a point per tier of the car, all or half; a wreck is
  patched up for free to 20 %), engine / tyres / armour upgrades (up to a car's own maximum levels: 2 on the
  Rustbucket, 5 on the Tyrant; the price of a level grows with the car's tier and the level), and equipment for the
  car: a spiked bumper and armour-piercing rounds. **The black market** sells mines ($100, up to 8), rockets ($250, up
  to 6) and nitro charges ($150, up to 6), and sabotage (a chosen rival of the next race starts at 55 % health). **The
  dealer** sells the six cars and takes the current one in part exchange for half of its price and of what was spent on
  it. **Standings** lists the ladder with every driver's car, points and a line about them.
- A simulated average player reaches the top in about 25-40 races, buying a new car every ten or so.
- The career saves itself before and after every race and when the garage is left; Save Career keeps it under a name.

A track is code (`TrackLayouts`): the control points of its centre line, its width (wider in hairpins, narrower in
passes), its laps, the line and the pickup clusters; the race builds it at run time (`TrackBuilder`: road, verges,
curbs, barriers with their colliders, ground, line and grid, scenery scattered from the look's props, landmarks, lamps)
in the look shown. Stars: three for a win on a track, two for second, one for third, in any race at this device.

## Several people

- **Split screen** (BaseGame's local play): two to four people, each in a view of their own with a HUD; options: the
  track (those opened on this device), the laps, the car (one of the six for everyone, or a random car per seat) and
  computer cars to fill the grid to four (none, easy, normal, hard). Race Again keeps the same people.
- **Online** (BaseGame's lobby and rooms; room options: the track, the laps, the car): a real time race like the Endless
  Runner's. Every device drives its own car and sends its pose about fifteen times a second; the others show as
  puppets that still fire, burn nitro and blow up from the flags of their poses. A car's own device judges every hit on
  it; mines, rockets and wrecks go through the room's action log, so every device has them; a pickup or a mine belongs
  to the first device that claims it; finishing is reported to the server, which ranks the room. When the first car
  finishes the others have thirty seconds. Private rooms, players at one device (companions) and tournaments are
  BaseGame's.
- **The server module** (`Server/Lib.cs`, database `skinnerboxes-deathrace`): the base server plus the race's tables
  (`RaceTrack`, `RaceRacer`, `RaceClaim`, `DeathRaceStats`) and reducers (`ClaimPickup`, `ClaimMine`, `FinishRace`);
  `Assets/Scripts/Online/OnlineRaceRules.cs` is compiled into the module and the game alike (payloads, pose flags, claim
  keys, scores). Publish it with BaseGame's `Gamebox > Server > Publish All Games` (or `spacetime publish` here).

Shared with the other games through BaseGame: the shop model and window (`Gamebox.Economy`, `ShopWindow`), the race
maths (`RacePath`, `LapTracker`, `RaceRanking`), the HUD pieces (`HudBar`, `Minimap`, `FloatingTexts`, `CameraShake`),
the low-poly mesh builder and palette (`LowPolyBuilder`, `PaletteTexture`) and the sound synthesiser (`SoundSynth`).

**Building**: `Death Race > Build Everything` (batch: `-executeMethod
Portfolio.DeathRace.EditorTools.DeathRaceBuildMenu.BuildEverything`) runs the art builder (surfaces, scenery,
interface, looks, and first the cars, items, effects and sounds), the prefab builder, the content builder (settings,
the nine campaign levels from `TrackLayouts`, the content asset, the two themes, the launcher's definition) and the
scene builder; the other items rebuild one step. After editing `Localization/DeathRace.csv`, run `Gamebox >
Localization > Set Up Project`. Preview tools render to PNG files: `TrackPreview.RenderAll`, `CarArt.ContactSheet`,
`DeathRaceArtPreview.BuildAndRender`.

**Tests** (`Tests/Editor`, edit mode): tracks (layouts, geometry, the builder), race rules, handling and the racing line,
combat rules and damage on real cars, the career (rules, ladder, offers, booking a race, the shop, a whole simulated
career, saves), the windows, split screen and online encoding.

**Tours** (development players): `-deathrace-tour <folder>` walks through the whole game offline (title, settings,
career and garage, a career race, results, quick race, pause, split screen with two and four people) and writes
screenshots and `tour.log`; `-deathrace-online host|join|hostprivate|joinprivate|hostparty|joinparty <folder> [name]`
plays an online race (run against a server of your own, `-gamebox-server http://127.0.0.1:<port>`); the checks
`-deathrace-drivecheck`, `-deathrace-combatcheck` and `-deathrace-careercheck` exercise driving and the AI, the fights,
and the career windows. Add `-gamebox-theme Nightfall` and `-gamebox-language he` for the other look and language.
